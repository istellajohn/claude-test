About · The instrument

Valence is decided at the outermost shell.

In chemistry, an atom’s valence electrons sit in its outer shell, and they are the only ones that form bonds. In psychology, valence is the charge of a feeling: the pull toward something, or away from it, that arrives before any reason does.

VALENCE works on both. We shape the outer layer of a person, a company or an idea, the part that meets strangers, and the charge it carries before anyone explains it.

Two meanings, one job

In chemistry

The part that bonds

An atom can have a dense, heavy core and still behave according to its outermost electrons. Most of who a company is lives in its core: its people, its product, its judgement. The market only ever touches the outer shell. If that layer is thin, crowded or out of date, nothing bonds, however much substance sits underneath.

In psychology

The charge before the reason

People decide whether they are drawn to something or wary of it in a fraction of a second, and spend the rest of the encounter justifying the decision. That first charge is rarely about information. It comes from tone, proportion, timing, who else is in the room and what the thing seems to refuse. It can be designed, and it can be neglected.

Why a perception practice keeps a journal

Most of our hours go into other people’s perception problems: founders read as smaller than their businesses, companies whose differentiation exists only in internal documents, artists whose audiences are still listening to old work.

The journal is where we do the opposite. We sit with someone long enough to see them in their current light, and publish what we find. It keeps our judgement honest. You cannot advise anyone on how they are read if you have stopped reading people closely yourself.
